# BFI-2 for Qualtrics

Ready-to-import [Qualtrics](https://www.qualtrics.com/) surveys for the **Big
Five Inventory-2** (BFI-2; Soto & John, 2017) and its short forms, with
automatic domain- and facet-level scoring built in.

The BFI-2 measures five personality domains — Extraversion, Agreeableness,
Conscientiousness, Negative Emotionality, and Open-Mindedness — each with three
facets.

## What's here

| File | Form | Items | Scores computed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `output/BFI-2_Full.qsf` | BFI-2 (full) | 60 | 5 domains + 15 facets |
| `output/BFI-2_Short.qsf` | BFI-2-S (short) | 30 | 5 domains + 15 facets |
| `output/BFI-2_ExtraShort.qsf` | BFI-2-XS (extra-short) | 15 | 5 domains |
| `output/BFI-2_Full_RPS.qsf` | BFI-2 (full) + Risk Propensity Scale | 68 | 5 domains + 15 facets + Risk Propensity + attention check |

Each file is a standalone survey with a working Qualtrics scoring
configuration, including correct reverse-coding of the 30 reverse-keyed items.
The extra-short form has no facet scores by design — its facets are single
items, for which the published BFI-2-XS defines domain scores only.

## Import into Qualtrics

1. In Qualtrics: **Projects → Create project → Survey → Import a QSF file**.
2. Choose one of the `output/*.qsf` files.
3. The survey and its scoring categories are created automatically. View them
   under **Survey → Scoring** (or the ⚙ scoring icon).

Responses export with one column per item plus one column per scoring category.
Scores are reported as **sums**; the standard BFI-2 convention is the *mean*
(divide a domain by its item count and a facet by its item count). The
`ForceResponse` on these matrices is a **soft** prompt — a respondent can
submit with an item skipped, and Qualtrics' scoring (`IgnoreNullValues:
true`) then sums only the items actually answered. Dividing that partial
sum by the *full* published item count understates the true mean for
that respondent; check for incomplete rows (or require answers) before
trusting a sum-to-mean conversion on data that may have missing items.

## Scoring

Scores use the official BFI-2 key (Soto & John, 2017): each domain is scored
from its 12 items (6 in the short form, 3 in the extra-short), each facet from
its 4 items (2 in the short form), with the 30 reverse-keyed items recoded.
The scoring is baked into the `.qsf` files, so no post-processing is needed
beyond the sum-to-mean conversion noted above.

The `output/` files were generated by the splitter tool in
`.claude/skills/bfi2-qsf-splitter/`, which injects the scoring by matching item
text against a master key verified against the official scoring documents. See
that directory for details.

`BFI-2_Full_RPS.qsf` additionally carries the 7-item Risk Propensity Scale
(Meertens & Lion, 2008) and one attention-check item, generated by that
same directory's `add_rps.py` on top of `BFI-2_Full.qsf`. It's the Part-2
survey for a 4-part Prolific longitudinal study hosted alongside a
separate platform (`ase2-ai-mode`) that auto-advances attentive,
consistent respondents to Part 3 and routes the rest to manual review —
see `docs/qualtrics-part2-wiring.md` for the Survey Flow wiring that
drives that decision (the BFI-2/RPS content and scoring here is
self-contained; that doc is only needed if you're standing up the same
real-time gate).

## Verifying response quality

`response_verification/verify_responses.py` scans a Qualtrics **response**
export (CSV or TSV, choice-label or numeric-value cells — all auto-detected) and
flags likely careless responding per participant, appending a `Careless_Flags`
column plus per-check metric and flag columns. It implements the post-hoc
screening battery recommended by Ward & Meade (2023) — through their "moderate"
tier — plus the BFI-2-specific DRIP scale (Ruchensky et al., 2025). The three
`output/BFI-2*.qsf` forms contain no instructed-response or bogus items and no
page timers, so a-priori methods don't apply to them (`BFI-2_Full_RPS.qsf` is
the exception — see above — but its attention-check item and client-side DRIP
score feed a separate real-time gate, not this offline script):

- **Speed** — total completion time under 2 seconds per item.
- **Longstring** — a run of ≥ 10 identical consecutive answers (straight-lining).
- **Within-person variability** — answer SD < 0.5 (catches patterned invariance
  longstring misses).
- **Mahalanobis distance** — multivariate outliers versus the sample centroid
  (χ² cutoff; skipped when there are too few responses for a stable covariance).
- **Psychometric synonyms** — within-person correlation across empirically
  paired items (sample r ≥ .60); low agreement flags inconsistency.
- **Even-odd consistency** — per-facet odd/even half scores correlated within
  person, Spearman-Brown corrected (Full and Short forms).
- **Person-total correlation** — each respondent against the leave-one-out
  sample consensus.
- **DRIP** (full form only) — the Detection of Response Inconsistency Procedure:
  the sum of absolute differences across 15 highly correlated BFI-2 item pairs
  (reverse-keyed first), flagged at ≥ 14 (Ruchensky et al., 2025).

Rows caught by the invariability checks are excluded from the reference
statistics the sample-based checks use (sequential removal), so straight-liners
don't distort the centroid the rest are judged against.

```
uv pip install -r response_verification/requirements.txt   # pandas, numpy, scipy
python3 response_verification/verify_responses.py export.csv -o flagged.csv
```

(Installs with [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/); plain `pip install` works too.)

Every threshold is overridable on the CLI; see
`response_verification/README.md` for the full check reference, threshold
provenance, and what's deliberately out of scope. Flagging is advisory, not an
error — every respondent is kept in the output; the flags mark rows worth
reviewing. Only this script needs the third-party packages in
`requirements.txt`; the surveys and the rest of the repo remain
dependency-free.

The methods come from:

> Ward, M. K., & Meade, A. W. (2023). Dealing with careless responding in
> survey data: Prevention, identification, and recommended best practices.
> *Annual Review of Psychology, 74*, 577–596.
> https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-040422-045007

> Ruchensky, J. R., Edens, J. F., & Donnellan, M. B. (2025). Development of an
> inconsistent responding scale for the Big Five Inventory-2. *Journal of
> Personality Assessment, 107*(3), 384–391.
> https://doi.org/10.1080/00223891.2024.2411557
